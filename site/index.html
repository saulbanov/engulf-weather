The short version

  • Expect afternoons around 72°, nights around 51°, and a chance of a shower or two. Day: T-shirt. Night: fleece.

  • In a normal Engulf week, afternoons run around 72° and nights around 45°; it rains on about 1 in 7 days, frost comes about 1 in 10 nights, and wind at burn time rarely reaches 15 mph. Day: T-shirt. Night: warm jacket, hat, gloves; winter layers in a cold year.
  • When you'll know more: the European forecast reaches burn night in the next day or two; the hour-by-hour forecast for burn night starts around Nov 7; from about Nov 11, three days out, forecasts are usually within a few degrees.

Why we think that

  • The latest forecasts from the European and US weather services make up 50% to 69% of each day's numbers. This far out they're rough, so the rest is what a normal Engulf week looks like, from 30 years of records.
  • NOAA's longer-range outlook leans slightly warmer than normal for Nov 7–13, so the normal-year part of Thursday and Friday is nudged that way.
  • The last month ran warm and dry, but that says little about any single day two weeks from now, so it isn't in the numbers.
  • Each update leans more on the forecast; from a week out it's all forecast.

How this estimate is built

Each day's numbers blend the forecast with a normal year for that date. Up to a week out the forecast counts fully; further out it counts less, down to half at two weeks. A normal year is nudged warmer, colder, wetter or drier when NOAA's outlook leans that way for the day.

Drought and river

The ground is dry. As of Oct 27, all of Tangipahoa Parish is in drought, and about 23% is in severe drought (US Drought Monitor). Dry spells are when parishes ban burning, and Engulf's rules say a ban can limit fire at the event. This page doesn't check for bans; the parish posts them at tangipahoa.org.

The Tangipahoa River near the site is about normal for this time of year. The event warns heavy rain can flood the site's roads.

Where this comes from
  • Forecasts: the European (ECMWF) and US (NOAA) weather services, plus the National Weather Service once burn night is within a week. Each service runs its forecast dozens of times with small changes; the chances here are how often those versions agree.
  • Longer range: NOAA's Climate Prediction Center outlook for 8 to 14 days ahead, which gives the odds of a warmer or colder, wetter or drier stretch than normal. The last 30 days: McComb's airport against its 1991-2020 normals.
  • Normal year: the same week of November over 1996-2025. Highs, lows and rain at the site are estimates from PRISM, a map built from nearby weather stations; wind and evening temperatures were measured at McComb, Mississippi, the nearest airport station, 26 km north.
  • Every download is kept, with its address, time and a fingerprint (hash), in the project's data/captures folder and data/manifest.jsonl.
  • The advice follows fixed rules of thumb, the same every time. It isn't Engulf's official word: the fire safety team decides what burns.
  • Not checked: burn bans, flooding on the site itself, and wind under the trees at camp.
