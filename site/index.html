Drought

100% of Tangipahoa Parish is in moderate drought or worse, 23% in severe (US Drought Monitor, 2026-10-27). Dry parishes issue burn bans, and Engulf's flame-effect policy says a ban can restrict fire at the event. Burn-ban status is not checked here: see tangipahoa.org.

Pack for the weekend

Planned for the cold end of the forecast runs and of the 30-year record.

  • Sleep: a bag rated 25°F or warmer, plus a pad (the cold comes up from the ground). The coldest likely night is about 35°F (in past years, 1 night in 10 went to 35°F or below). Generators are off 10 pm to 10 am, so no plug-in heat.
  • Burn night and evenings: a warm jacket, hat and something for your hands. Evenings can feel as cold as 45°F; in past years 1 evening in 10 felt 36°F or colder, so a hat and gloves earn their space.
  • Afternoons: a t-shirt, sun hat and sunscreen. Afternoons can reach 78°F, and shade is limited.
  • Rain: a rain shell and mud boots whatever the forecast. The event asks for both, and 23% of runs show rain on at least one day.
  • Dew: dry bags or a bin for clothes and electronics; in past years 34% of calm predawn hours came within 3°F of the dew point, which wets everything without rain.
  • Water: 1.5 gallons a person a day (the event's number); more if afternoons are warm.

How this is made

  • Forecasts: ECMWF's 51-run ensemble and NOAA's 31-run GEFS (via Open-Meteo), and the National Weather Service hourly forecast. Shares are of runs, not promises; past a week, the spread is the honest forecast.
  • Usual: PRISM 4 km daily highs, lows and rain at the site (modelled), and McComb, MS airport hourly wind and temperature 26 km north (measured), Nov 9–18, 1996–2025.
  • Advice comes from fixed thresholds, not judgement. The Fire Art Response Team and burn leads decide what burns; this page is not Engulf's.
  • Not checked: burn bans, the site's own drainage, wind among the trees at camp.
  • Every download is kept and hashed: data · this page as JSON · code.
