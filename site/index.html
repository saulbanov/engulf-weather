Burn night

Saturday: temple around 8 pm, effigy around 10 pm.

Light wind (around 5 mph), mostly dry (1 in 10 chance of rain), and about 54°F by midnight, colder when you're standing still. A thin crescent moon (28% lit), so a dark sky.

Fire note: the parish is in drought. Dry spells are when parishes ban burning, and Engulf's rules say a ban can limit fire at the event. This page doesn't track bans; check tangipahoa.org.

An hour-by-hour forecast for burn night appears here about a week before, around Nov 7.

Pack

  • Sleeping bag rated 25°F or warmer, and a sleeping pad
    Nights could drop to about 36°F. Generators are off 10 pm to 10 am, so no plug-in heaters.
  • Headlamp, plus lights for your camp, tent lines and bike
    It's dark by 5:05 pm: about 13 hours of darkness a night.
  • A warm jacket, hat and something for your hands
    For evenings and the burns. With the wind it could feel like 45°F.
  • Rain jacket and mud boots
    The event asks for both whatever the forecast; there's about a 2 in 10 chance of rain on at least one day.
  • Sun hat and sunscreen
    Afternoons could reach 78°F, and there isn't much shade.
  • Dry bags or a plastic bin
    Heavy dew on calm nights soaks anything left out, even without rain.
  • Thermos
    Something hot for standing out at the burns.
  • 4½ gallons of water per person
    The event's figure: 1.5 gallons a day for three days. Nothing is sold on site.
  • Cash for ice
    Ice is the only thing sold: noon to 4 pm, Thursday to Saturday.

Day by day

Thu Nov 12 · arrival and setup; gates 10 am to 10 pm

  • Afternoon (near 70°F): a t-shirt, with a long-sleeve shirt handy.
  • After dark (feels near 56°F): a fleece or sweater under a light jacket.
  • Overnight (low near 51°F): sleep in a dry base layer and socks.
  • Before you come: eat on the road. Gates open at 10 am and setup eats the afternoon.
  • Dinner: something easy and pre-made. It's dark by 5:06 pm, so get camp up first.

Fri Nov 13 · the full day on site

  • Afternoon (near 70°F): a t-shirt, with a long-sleeve shirt handy.
  • After dark (feels near 56°F): a fleece or sweater under a light jacket.
  • Overnight (low near 50°F): sleep in a dry base layer and socks.

Sat Nov 14 · burn night: temple around 8 pm, effigy around 10 pm

  • Afternoon (near 72°F): a t-shirt, with a long-sleeve shirt handy.
  • After dark (feels near 54°F): a fleece or sweater under a light jacket.
  • Overnight (low near 46°F): sleep in a dry base layer and socks.
  • Dinner: a real meal by 6:30 pm. You'll be standing out from the temple burn at 8 through the effigy at 10.

Sun Nov 15 · exodus: off the land by 5 pm

  • Afternoon (near 66°F): long sleeves and a light layer.
  • Breakfast: something hot. Mornings near 46°F make coffee and oats worth it (a stove cleared with your camp; personal fires are not allowed).
  • Eat the cooler down; you have to be off the land by 5 pm.

Drought and river

The ground is dry. As of Oct 27, all of Tangipahoa Parish is in drought, and about 23% is in severe drought (US Drought Monitor). Dry spells are when parishes ban burning, and Engulf's rules say a ban can limit fire at the event. This page doesn't check for bans; the parish posts them at tangipahoa.org.

The Tangipahoa River near the site is about normal for this time of year. The event warns heavy rain can flood the site's roads.

Where this comes from
  • Forecasts: the European (ECMWF) and US (NOAA) weather services, plus the National Weather Service once burn night is within a week. Each service runs its forecast dozens of times with small changes; the chances here are how often those versions agree.
  • Normal year: the same week of November over the past 30 years, at the site and at McComb, Mississippi, the nearest airport weather station.
  • The advice follows fixed rules of thumb, the same every time. It isn't Engulf's official word: the fire safety team decides what burns.
  • Not checked: burn bans, flooding on the site itself, and wind under the trees at camp.
